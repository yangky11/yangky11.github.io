I lead a research team at Apodex working on large language models (LLMs) for mathematics and verification. AI systems generate code and proofs faster than anyone can check them, and as generation gets cheaper, verification becomes the bottleneck. Our work is on closing that gap: models and systems that verify outputs cheaply and reliably, so the human review needed to trust them keeps shrinking. Mathematics is where this is furthest along, because correctness there can be verified relatively easily — but the same principle applies wherever outputs can be checked against evidence, including the training infrastructure AI itself runs on.

What we work on

  • Mathematical capabilities of LLMs. We work across the training stack rather than at a single stage — data, mid-training, post-training, and agents — because no one stage creates mathematical ability, and each stage bounds what the next can add.
  • Verification. Two directions: formal guarantees for the infrastructure that training runs on, and verifiers that tell an agent whether it is still on track. The longer the task, the more errors compound, and the more a cheap, reliable check is worth.

Background

I have worked on machine learning for formal mathematics since 2018, beginning with CoqGym, a learning environment for the Coq proof assistant, and later LeanDojo and Lean Copilot, which connect language models to Lean and are in regular use in that community.
